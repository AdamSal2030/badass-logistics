Step-Deck Trailer Dimensions: Deck Height, Max Load Height & Ground Clearance

Every number you need before you book a step deck — deck heights, usable lengths, legal cargo height, and the ground clearance question nobody answers straight.

FIELD GUIDE BY THE CREW ✓
REAL LOADS — REAL NUMBERS NO FLUFF, JUST SPECS FROM THE DISPATCH DESK

Trailers · By the Badass Logistics crew · September 9, 2026

// quick answer

A standard 53-foot step-deck trailer has a 41-foot lower deck sitting about 41 inches (3 ft 5 in) off the ground and an 11-to-12-foot upper deck at roughly 60 inches. Legal cargo height on the lower deck is about 10 feet before you need a permit, versus roughly 8 feet 6 inches on a flatbed. Payload runs about 48,000 lbs on a tandem.

Most step-deck questions are really one question: will my load go down the road legally without a permit? That comes down to deck height, and step-deck deck height is the whole reason the trailer exists.

What is a step-deck truck?

A step-deck — also called a drop deck — is a flatbed with two levels. The front section sits at standard flatbed height to clear the tractor's fifth wheel. Behind that, the deck steps down closer to the ground. That step is worth about a foot and a half of cargo height, and a foot and a half is the difference between a routine legal move and a permitted oversize load.

There is no curtain, no roof, no walls. It loads from the top with a crane, from the side with a forklift, or up ramps from the back. It is the workhorse for freight that is too tall for a flatbed but nowhere near tall enough to justify an RGN.

Those are typical numbers, not universal ones. Deck heights vary an inch or two by manufacturer, by suspension, and by how worn the tires are. If your load is within two inches of legal, measure the actual trailer — do not trust a spec sheet, and do not trust this one.

Step-deck max height, explained properly

The federal legal height limit on most routes is 13 feet 6 inches from pavement to the highest point of the load. Some western states allow 14 feet. Your usable cargo height is that limit minus the deck height.

  • Lower deck at 41 inches: 13'6" minus 3'5" leaves roughly 10 feet 1 inch of cargo height.
  • Upper deck at 60 inches: 13'6" minus 5'0" leaves roughly 8 feet 6 inches — the same as a standard flatbed.
  • Standard flatbed at 60 inches: roughly 8 feet 6 inches.

So the honest way to say it: a step deck does not make your load shorter, it lowers the floor underneath it by about 19 inches. Everything above 10 feet on the lower deck is permit territory, and past roughly 12 feet you are looking at a double-drop or stretch trailer instead.

What about ground clearance?

This is the question that gets asked constantly and answered badly, because "ground clearance" means two different things.

Deck height is how far the loading surface sits off the pavement — about 41 inches on the lower deck. That is the number you use for height math.

Actual under-trailer clearance — the gap between the lowest structural point of the trailer and the road — is much smaller, usually somewhere around 5 to 8 inches under the frame rails when loaded. That is the number that matters for steep driveway aprons, railroad crossings, speed bumps, and loading-dock ramps.

A loaded step deck will drag on a sharp break-over that a flatbed clears without a thought. If your pickup or delivery has a steep apron, say so before the truck is dispatched. Fixing it at the gate means a second trip.

When a step deck is the wrong call

  • Load under 8'6" tall. A standard flatbed hauls it for less. Do not pay for deck you are not using.
  • Load over about 10 feet. You want an RGN or a double-drop, which put the well even lower.
  • Load over 48,000 lbs. Now it is an axle problem, not a height problem — that is multi-axle territory.
  • Weather-sensitive freight. A Conestoga gives you a rolling curtain instead of a tarp crew.

Frequently asked questions

What is the max height on a step deck trailer?
On the lower deck, roughly 10 feet of cargo height before you need an oversize permit — that is the 13'6" legal limit minus about 41 inches of deck height. On the upper deck it drops to about 8 feet 6 inches. Some western states allow 14 feet overall, which buys you another six inches.
How tall is a step deck trailer deck?
The lower deck sits about 41 inches (3 ft 5 in) off the ground and the upper deck about 60 inches (5 ft). Exact heights vary an inch or two by manufacturer, suspension, and tire wear, so measure the actual trailer if your load is borderline.
What is the ground clearance of a step deck trailer?
Two different numbers. Deck height — the loading surface — is about 41 inches on the lower deck. Actual clearance under the frame is much less, typically 5 to 8 inches loaded, which is what matters for steep driveway aprons and railroad crossings.
Is a step deck the same as a drop deck?
Yes. Step deck and drop deck are the same trailer under two names. A double drop is a different trailer with a lowered well between the axles for taller freight.
